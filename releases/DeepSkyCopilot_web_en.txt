DeepSkyCopilot - Highlights for the website
(what is worth highlighting in each published release, in plain language; most recent first)

2026-11-10
- **Cleaner photos, night after night.** The app learns how your telescope responds and shifts each shot just enough to keep sensor defects from showing, without letting the framing slowly drift away.

2026-11-07
- **Pick your target on a sky map.** Move around the sky like in a planetarium, with real photos, and choose what to photograph with one click. You see the exact framing before you start.
- **Know what is worth shooting each night.** Every object gets a score from 0 to 100 based on its altitude, the Moon, the time available and the clouds, and the list is sorted from best to worst.
- **Tonight's plan, almost done for you.** The app suggests the best objects of the night and can fill the night for you, one after another with no overlaps.

2026-11-06
- **Cloud forecast while planning.** Before choosing, the app tells you whether the sky will be clear during each object's time span and offers to shoot only the clear stretch.
- **Control the night from your phone.** Start and stop the queue and do the polar alignment from your phone's browser, without carrying the computer next to the telescope.
- **Simpler polar alignment.** An arrow and the deviation in degrees, green when it is under 1°, like in the Seestar app.

2026-11-02
- **In English and Spanish.** The app and its manual open in your computer's language.
- **Stacking when the night ends.** When it finishes, DeepSkyCopilot asks DeepSkyForce to stack each target and leaves the result ready in a folder.
